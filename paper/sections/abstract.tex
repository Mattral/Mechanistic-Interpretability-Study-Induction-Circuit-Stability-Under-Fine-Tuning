\begin{abstract}
We ask whether induction head circuits in attention-only transformers remain
structurally stable under fine-tuning on a narrow distribution, and at what
training step any structural change first occurs.
%
We fine-tune the two-layer \texttt{attn-only-2l} transformer on 500{,}000 tokens
of Python code and a matched TinyStories prose control, measuring per-head
induction scores and activation-patching attribution at every 100-step checkpoint
across three random seeds; at initialisation, the documented induction head
L1H6 has a prefix-matching score of $0.408 \pm 0.103$ and an
activation-patching attribution of $0.952$, both consistent with prior
characterisations of this model.
%
\textbf{Finding (preliminary, seed 42; seeds 123 and 7 trained but not yet
analysed):} We find that L1H6's prefix-matching score \emph{increases} ---
rather than degrades --- under both code and prose fine-tuning, from $0.408$
at initialisation to $0.602$ (code) and $0.583$ (prose) after 100 steps, and
to $0.650$ and $0.617$ respectively after 200 steps, with the largest change
occurring in the first 100 steps and a similar magnitude in both conditions
--- a result not previously reported for this model --- which implies that,
at this token budget, fine-tuning on a narrow distribution does not silently
erode the induction circuit and may instead reinforce it regardless of
domain.
%
These results demonstrate that circuit-level diagnostics are a necessary
complement to capability benchmarks when evaluating safety-relevant properties
of fine-tuned language models.
\end{abstract}
