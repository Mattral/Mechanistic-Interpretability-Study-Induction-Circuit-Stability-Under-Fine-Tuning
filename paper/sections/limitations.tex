\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Single seed analysed.}
All fine-tuning results reported in Section~\ref{sec:results} are from seed
42 only. Seeds 123 and 7 were trained (checkpoints exist for both conditions)
but not yet swept and analysed at the time of writing. The reported deltas
($+0.194$/$+0.048$ for code, $+0.175$/$+0.034$ for prose) should be treated
as a single-seed preliminary observation pending confirmation; no
$\pm 1$ SD across seeds is yet available for the fine-tuning trajectories
(unlike the baseline induction score, which is averaged over 100 sequences
and does carry a standard deviation, $0.408 \pm 0.103$).

\textbf{Three checkpoints per run.}
With \texttt{checkpoint\_every=100} and 244 total steps (500K tokens at
batch size 16, sequence length 128), each run produces checkpoints at steps
0, 100, and 200---three points per trajectory. The Savitzky-Golay smoothing
window used for phase detection (DECISION-004, window$=5$) exceeds this
series length and falls back to raw consecutive differences. A longer token
budget or more frequent checkpointing would allow genuine smoothing and a
finer-grained transition analysis.

\textbf{Model scope.}
All experiments use a 2-layer attention-only transformer.
Claims are scoped to this model class; production models with MLP layers may differ.

\textbf{Circuit threshold.}
The attribution threshold of 0.5 is arbitrary.
Raw scores are reported in all figures to allow alternative thresholds.

\textbf{Token budget.}
500K tokens may not capture changes emerging at larger budgets; the
front-loaded transition pattern observed here (most change in the first 100
steps) raises the question of whether longer runs would show further change,
a plateau, or reversal.

\textbf{Dataset representativeness.}
Results may differ for other programming languages, narrower Python subsets,
or other prose corpora.

\textbf{Causal completeness.}
Activation patching measures total head contribution; sub-head decomposition
(individual attention patterns vs.\ full output) is not performed.

\textbf{Adversarial probe edge case.}
The \texttt{all\_same\_token} probe (Figure~\ref{fig:adv}) produces a
normalised logit difference of $2.35$, exceeding the clean baseline of
$1.0$. We interpret this as a degenerate case: when every token in the
sequence is identical, the prefix-matching pattern is trivially satisfied at
every position, and the normalisation (which divides by a clean-vs-corrupted
logit difference computed on non-degenerate sequences) does not account for
this. This probe result should not be read as evidence the fine-tuned model
exceeds "clean" induction performance in any meaningful sense; it reflects a
limitation of the normalisation scheme on degenerate inputs.
