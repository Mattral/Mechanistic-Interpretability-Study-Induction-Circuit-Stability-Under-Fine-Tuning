\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have presented the first systematic characterisation of induction circuit stability
under fine-tuning in two-layer attention-only transformers.
By measuring per-head induction scores and activation-patching attribution at every
100-step checkpoint across both a code and a prose condition, we provide a replicable
methodology for circuit-level monitoring during fine-tuning.

\textbf{Summary (preliminary, seed 42).} The induction circuit in
\texttt{attn-only-2l} consists of a single head, L1H6 (prefix-matching score
$0.408 \pm 0.103$, attribution $0.952$), consistent with prior
characterisations of this model. Under both Python code and TinyStories
prose fine-tuning, this head's prefix-matching score \emph{increased} over
244 steps---from $0.408$ to $0.650$ (code) and to $0.617$ (prose)---with the
largest change occurring in the first 100 steps in both conditions. At this
token budget and for this seed, we find no evidence of circuit dissolution;
if anything, fine-tuning reinforces this head's prefix-matching behaviour
regardless of domain. Confirming this across the remaining two seeds
(123, 7, already trained but not yet swept) is the immediate next step.

The interactive visualisation dashboard and full codebase are released alongside this
paper, enabling practitioners to apply circuit-level monitoring to their own
fine-tuning pipelines.

\textbf{Future work.}
(1)~Complete the 3-seed sweep for both conditions to confirm the
reinforcement direction and obtain confidence intervals.
(2)~Extend the token budget beyond 500K to test whether the apparent
plateau between steps 100 and 200 continues, reverses, or accelerates.
(3)~Extend to GPT-2 small (with MLP layers) using automated circuit
discovery~\citep{conmy2023automated}.
(4)~Investigate whether reinforcement of prefix-matching behaviour during
fine-tuning has any measurable effect on susceptibility to
repetition-based or prompt-injection attacks that exploit induction-like
heuristics.
