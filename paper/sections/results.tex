\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Baseline Replication}
Figure~\ref{fig:ind-baseline} shows per-head induction (prefix-matching)
scores in the pretrained model, computed via
$\text{IS}(l,h) = \frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n-2} A^{(l,h)}[n+j, j+1]$
(Eq.~\ref{eq:is}; $n=30$, 100 sequences, seed 42).
Head L1H6 has the highest score by a wide margin ($0.408 \pm 0.103$),
consistent with prior characterisations of \texttt{attn-only-2l} identifying
L1H6 as its canonical induction head \citep{olsson2022context}.
Figure~\ref{fig:attr-baseline} shows the activation-patching attribution
scores; L1H6 has attribution $0.952$, the only head exceeding the
$\geq 0.5$ circuit-membership threshold (DECISION-002).
Figure~\ref{fig:circuit} shows the resulting circuit diagram: a single
layer-1 head (L1H6) constitutes the induction circuit at this threshold; no
layer-0 head meets the threshold as a "previous-token" partner under the
activation-patching protocol used here, though L0H7 has the highest layer-0
prefix-matching score ($\approx 0.005$, near the noise floor).
Figure~\ref{fig:attn-heatmap} visualises L1H6's attention pattern on a
repeated-token sequence: the head attends along a diagonal offset by the
sequence period, the signature pattern of prefix matching.

\subsection{Code Fine-Tuning}
\textbf{Preliminary result (seed 42 only; seeds 123 and 7 trained but not
yet swept --- see Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).}
Figure~\ref{fig:ind-code} shows L1H6's induction score over 244 fine-tuning
steps (3 checkpoints: 0, 100, 200) on Python code. The score
\emph{increases} from $0.408$ at step 0 to $0.602$ at step 100
($\Delta = +0.194$) and to $0.650$ at step 200 ($\Delta = +0.048$ from step
100). Both changes from step 0 exceed the $\geq 0.1$ meaningful-change
threshold (DECISION-004). The circuit does not dissolve under code
fine-tuning at this token budget; if anything, the prefix-matching behaviour
of L1H6 strengthens.

\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig5_induction_code.pdf}
  \caption{Induction score vs.\ training step, Python code fine-tuning,
    seed 42. L1H6 (highlighted) increases from $0.408 \to 0.602 \to 0.650$.
    All other heads remain near zero throughout.}
  \label{fig:ind-code}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Prose Control}
\textbf{Preliminary result (seed 42 only).}
Figure~\ref{fig:ind-prose} shows the matched TinyStories prose control.
L1H6's induction score increases from $0.408$ to $0.583$ at step 100
($\Delta = +0.175$) and to $0.617$ at step 200 ($\Delta = +0.034$ from step
100) --- the same qualitative direction as the code condition, with a
slightly smaller step-100 increase ($+0.175$ vs.\ $+0.194$). At this
single-seed, 3-checkpoint resolution, the code and prose trajectories are
not clearly distinguishable; both show the circuit strengthening rather than
degrading.

\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig6_induction_prose.pdf}
  \caption{Induction score vs.\ training step, TinyStories prose
    (control), seed 42. L1H6 (highlighted) increases from
    $0.408 \to 0.583 \to 0.617$, closely tracking the code condition
    (Figure~\ref{fig:ind-code}).}
  \label{fig:ind-prose}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Phase Transition Analysis}
Figure~\ref{fig:phase} shows L1H6's trajectory under code fine-tuning with
the detected transition. With only 3 checkpoints, the Savitzky-Golay
smoothing window (DECISION-004, window $=5$) exceeds the series length and
falls back to raw consecutive differences; one transition is detected at
step 100 ($\Delta = +0.194$) for both conditions (Table~\ref{tab:phase}).
No transition is detected between steps 100 and 200 in either condition
($|\Delta| < 0.1$), suggesting the largest change occurs early in training
and the score partially plateaus by step 200.

\subsection{Adversarial Probes}
Figure~\ref{fig:adv} shows normalised logit differences for the
post-fine-tuning model (code, seed 42, step 200) across the 20-probe suite,
relative to the clean-repeated baseline ($=1.0$) and a no-induction reference
($=0.0$). Probes that preserve the prefix-matching structure with minor
perturbations (\texttt{sub\_pos\_*}, \texttt{sub\_end\_*}, \texttt{multi\_sub\_*})
retain $0.82$--$1.00$ of the clean score, indicating the fine-tuned circuit
is robust to small positional and substitution perturbations. Probes that
remove the repeated-prefix structure (\texttt{reversed\_second\_half},
\texttt{cyclic\_shift\_1/3}, \texttt{fully\_random}) drop to $0.09$--$0.18$,
confirming the head's behaviour is specifically tied to the prefix-matching
pattern rather than a generic high-attention bias. The
\texttt{all\_same\_token} probe is an outlier at $2.35\times$ the clean
score --- a degenerate case (every token identical) where the attention
pattern is technically satisfied by every position simultaneously, discussed
in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig8_adversarial.pdf}
  \caption{Adversarial probe results, post fine-tuning (code, seed 42, step
    200). Dashed green line: clean-repeated baseline ($1.0$). Structure-preserving
    probes cluster near $1.0$; structure-removing probes drop to $0.09$--$0.18$.
    \texttt{all\_same\_token} is a degenerate outlier at $2.35$.}
  \label{fig:adv}
\end{figure}
