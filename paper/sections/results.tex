\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Baseline Replication}

Figure~\ref{fig:ind-baseline} shows per-head induction (prefix-matching)
scores for the pretrained model ($n=30$, 100 sequences, seed 42).
Head L1H6 is the clear outlier at $0.408 \pm 0.103$; the next highest
head (L1H7) scores $0.033$ --- a $12.4\times$ separation that unambiguously
identifies L1H6 as the sole induction head.
All layer-0 heads score below $0.006$, consistent with their role as
previous-token (not induction) heads \citep{olsson2022context}.

Figure~\ref{fig:attr-baseline} confirms L1H6's causal primacy: activation
patching attribution $= 0.952$, the only head exceeding the pre-registered
$\geq 0.5$ circuit-membership threshold (DECISION-002).
All other heads have attribution $< 0.053$.
Figure~\ref{fig:circuit} shows the resulting single-head circuit diagram.
Figure~\ref{fig:attn-heatmap} shows L1H6's attention pattern on a
repeated-token sequence: the characteristic off-diagonal stripe of
prefix-matching is clearly visible, with each token in the second half
attending to the position following its first occurrence.

\subsection{Code Fine-Tuning}

\textbf{Note on data:} Seed 42 only. Seeds 123 and 7 were trained but
checkpoints were lost to Colab session resets before sweeping; re-running
those seeds is the immediate next step (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

Figure~\ref{fig:ind-code} shows L1H6's trajectory over 244 fine-tuning
steps on Python code (Table~\ref{tab:main-results}).
The prefix-matching score \emph{increases} monotonically from $0.408$ at
step 0 to $0.584$ at step 100 ($\Delta = +0.176$) and $0.638$ at step 200
($\Delta_\text{cumulative} = +0.230$).
The score crosses the $0.5$ IS threshold between steps 0 and 100 and
continues rising in the second interval.
By step 200 the circuit is operating at $1.56\times$ its pretrained
prefix-matching strength.

Concurrently, the induction task cross-entropy loss decreases from $11.848$
(step 0) to $8.433$ (step 100) to $7.557$ (step 200), and the clean
logit difference increases from $4.631$ to $6.859$ to $7.566$,
confirming that the output improvement is real and not merely a change
in attention routing.

\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering

  \caption{L1H6 prefix-matching score vs.\ training step, Python code
    fine-tuning (seed 42, \texttt{transformersbook/codeparrot}).
    The circuit strengthens monotonically; both the $0.5$ IS threshold
    (dashed) crossing and the subsequent plateau are visible.}
  \label{fig:ind-code}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Prose Control}

Figure~\ref{fig:ind-prose} shows the TinyStories prose control.

\subsection{Code vs.\ Prose Comparison}

Both conditions produce near-identical IS trajectories at step 100
($\Delta\text{code} - \Delta\text{prose} = +0.001$), diverging slightly
by step 200 ($\Delta\text{code} - \Delta\text{prose} = +0.022$, code
slightly higher). At the single-seed, 3-checkpoint resolution available,
these differences are not statistically distinguishable; confirming
whether code fine-tuning produces a reliably larger IS increase than
prose fine-tuning requires the remaining two seeds.

The qualitative conclusion is robust: \textbf{neither condition degrades
the induction circuit}. If anything, both conditions reinforce it.

\subsection{Phase Transition Analysis}

Figure~\ref{fig:phase} shows L1H6's trajectory with the detected
transition. A single transition is detected at step 100 in both conditions
(Table~\ref{tab:phase}), driven by the $>0.1$ threshold (DECISION-004).
No additional transition is detected between steps 100 and 200
($|\Delta| = 0.054$ for code, $0.033$ for prose, both below $0.1$),
indicating that the largest change is concentrated in the first half of
training and the score partially plateaus thereafter.

\subsection{Adversarial Probes}

Figure~\ref{fig:adv} shows the 22-probe adversarial results for the
post-fine-tuning model (code, step 200).
Table~\ref{tab:adv} summarises the key probe categories.

\textbf{Structure-preserving probes} (\texttt{sub\_pos\_*},
\texttt{sub\_end\_*}, \texttt{multi\_sub\_*}, \texttt{clean\_repeated}):
post-fine-tuning mean $= 0.944 \pm 0.043$, nearly unchanged from
pre-fine-tuning ($0.946 \pm 0.043$). The circuit is robust to minor
positional and substitution perturbations after fine-tuning.

\textbf{Structure-removing probes} (\texttt{reversed\_second\_half},
\texttt{cyclic\_shift\_1}, \texttt{cyclic\_shift\_3},
\texttt{fully\_random}): post-fine-tuning mean $= 0.132 \pm 0.030$,
confirming that the head's behaviour is specifically tied to the
prefix-matching structure, not a generic high-attention bias.

\textbf{Degenerate outlier} (\texttt{all\_same\_token}): pre $= 4.123$,
post $= 2.414$ ($\Delta = -1.709$). As noted in
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}, this probe is degenerate; when every token
is identical, every position simultaneously satisfies the prefix-matching
criterion, which is why the normalised logit difference exceeds $1.0$.
Notably, this outlier \emph{decreases} after fine-tuning, suggesting the
model becomes less susceptible to this degenerate pattern --- a
qualitatively positive result.
